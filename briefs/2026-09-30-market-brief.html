Global Macro Themes & Market Transmission
Energy & Agricultural Commodities: The Primary Disinflation Anchor
WTI Crude ($71.15) · Non-OPEC Production · Global Grain Yields
 🟢 COST ANCHOR
What's Happening in the World: WTI crude oil has settled near $71/barrel and agricultural grains remain abundant, driven by record production from non-OPEC producers (United States, Guyana, Brazil) that fully counters OPEC+ supply discipline.
Impact on Bonds & Equities: Suppressed fuel and food inputs completely remove the risk of 1970s-style second-wave cost-push inflation. This enables central banks to execute rate cuts without hesitation, protects corporate operating margins in transportation and retail, and keeps 5-year inflation breakevens anchored at 2.15%.
💡 What this means:
When oil stays near $71 and grain harvests are abundant, gas and groceries don't get more expensive. In the 1970s, oil spikes caused runaway inflation that crushed stocks. Today, the US and South America are pumping so much oil that prices stay low even with Middle East conflicts. That gives the Federal Reserve the green light to keep lowering interest rates, which helps consumers spend and helps companies grow profits.

The Bond Market: Yield Curve Normalization & Multiple Expansion
10Y Yield (4.18%) · 2Y/10Y Spread (+53 bps) · Equity Discount Rates
 🔵 RATE NORMALIZATION
What's Happening in the World: The 2Y/10Y Treasury yield curve has normalized to a positive +53 bps upward slope after the longest inversion in financial history (26 months). Benchmark 10Y yields have anchored comfortably at 4.18%.
Impact on Bonds & Equities: A steepening, non-inverted curve restores commercial bank net interest margins (XLF +1.1% 7D), eliminates the artificial recession overhang, and compresses the equity discount rate, allowing S&P 500 P/E multiples to hold firm without triggering valuation compression.
💡 What this means:
For over two years, the bond market was 'inverted' (short-term interest rates were higher than long-term rates), which in the past almost always predicted a recession. Now, that inversion has completely fixed itself—long-term rates are safely higher than short-term rates again. That means the bond market is giving the green light for steady economic expansion rather than sounding the alarm for a sudden downturn.
